# daftar — the steward's charter

This charter binds the one who stewards daftar, and whoever joins the stewardship; like the manifesto, it binds no one
else (manifesto: binds). It is read with `MANIFESTO.md`, which says what daftar holds to, and `LICENSE`, which says
what anyone may do with daftar.

## 1. The steward

The steward of daftar is known as **Omid**; the state's registers name him **Ahmad Kordbacheh**; he publishes as
**0mit**. The three names are one person (manifesto: who-where). Where a law asks for the registered name, that one is
used; everywhere else, the name he is known by.

The steward ratifies daftar's law and this charter, keeps the name, and answers for daftar.

## 2. What the steward answers for

What the steward publishes as daftar — its releases, its repository, its pages — has a person behind it. A claim that
it infringes a right, or breaks the law of a place, may be addressed to the steward at omid@0mit.com, or through the
contacts published at 0mit.com while he holds that domain, by the order on the ground — the courts and authorities of the places daftar reaches — or by anyone who holds a right. He will answer it,
cure what is found wrong, and bear the responsibility the law in force places on him for what he himself published.
He does not answer for a garden, which is its gardener's, nor for a copy or a work that someone else changes or
publishes.

This is a commitment to be reachable and to answer. It is not a warranty, an indemnity or an assumption of liability,
nor a statement or agreement in writing under sections 15 and 16 of the GNU AGPL or section 5 of CC BY 4.0. Every
disclaimer and limitation in the licences stands, as far as the law allows.

## 3. The covenant of the difference — 1:1:1

The gain is what daftar saves on the steward's own work: money received, or money he no longer spends, measured
against what the same work cost him before, after the taxes it bears. It is recorded in his garden, so every share
shows its path. The steward keeps none of it (manifesto: gain, cost). It is divided in three equal shares:

- **one third for the good of efficiency** — returned to those who do the work and those who buy it, and to making
  the work more efficient still;
- **one third for keeping the base** — the standard, the tools and their upkeep, and what the tools were paid for by:
  the world's shared knowledge and the earth's resources;
- **one third held for the right on the ground** — kept and recorded against any right that someone holds, under the
  law that applies where it is claimed, and that daftar as the steward publishes it infringes, once that right is
  established against the steward by a settlement he signs or by a final decision of a court or authority. What is
  paid from it is paid toward what that right is owed, not in addition to it. Where several such rights are
  established, it is shared in proportion to what each is owed. Holding it is prudence, not an admission that any such
  right exists. It is held without end: what established rights do not take stays held and recorded, never passes to
  the other two thirds, and passes with the stewardship.

A payment that sanctions, currency rules or the bank that would carry it forbid is not made, and is never routed
around them; it stays held and recorded. This covenant is the steward's own undertaking, and binds whoever joins the
stewardship. It is not a promise of reward to the public, and gives no one a claim in law. To everyone else who gains
by daftar, it is an invitation.

## 4. Patents

The steward irrevocably promises, for himself and for anyone to whom he transfers a patent, not to assert any patent
he owns or controls, now or later, against making, using, selling, offering or distributing an implementation of any
version of daftar's law he has published, whether conformant or not, or against using, modifying or conveying
daftar's code. The promise is suspended toward anyone who asserts a patent against daftar, its steward or its users
over daftar. Contributors to the law make the same promise; contributors to the code grant the licence of section 11
of the GNU AGPL (`CONTRIBUTING.md`).

## 5. The name

The word daftar belongs to everyone, and what may present itself as daftar is the manifesto's (manifesto: name). The
licences give no right to present a work as this project's; a work that may not may say it is based on daftar.
Before the steward says a work does not conform, its maker is heard (manifesto: heard), and the gate at a pinned
version decides. The name is not a registered mark. It stands on the licences' own terms — CC BY 4.0 grants no
endorsement and asks that a change be indicated; the code carries the additional terms in `LICENSE` — and on the law
against unfair competition where there is one. The name and its conformance are never sold (manifesto: cost).

## 6. Agreements and undertakings, kept in daftar's own law

The licences, the undertakings of this charter, a contributor's sign-off, a person's consent to hold their words, and
an acceptance between gardens are each kept as daftar keeps any agreement: a `contract` bean with its parties, the
words they agreed in, and its clauses, each clause permitted or forbidden (manifesto: checked). Its words stand in the
law, why they are so in the reasoning, its ratification in the journal, and every acceptance in history (manifesto:
layers). The steward's undertakings here are kept in the steward's garden.

## 7. How this charter changes

This charter changes as the manifesto does (manifesto: changes): by the steward's ratification, in the commit that
makes the change, marked RULE-CHANGE; and a share already held stays held. A steward who breaks the
manifesto or this charter is no longer its steward (manifesto: binds).
