# daftar — its manifesto

> «φιλοσόφου ἢ φιλοκάλου ἢ μουσικοῦ τινος καὶ ἐρωτικοῦ» — a lover of wisdom, or of beauty, or one of the Muses'
> people and of love: the first of the nine lives (*Phaedrus* 248d).
>
> «ἐν γράμμασι κήπους» — gardens in letters (*Phaedrus* 276d).

## The stance

### serve
daftar serves the Muses — knowledge, and the art of keeping it — and is never the sophist, who sells the appearance of wisdom, nor the tyrant, who closes what belongs to all.

### cold
What daftar keeps is written to be read correctly cold — on paper, years later, by a reader of any make — so legibility and provenance come before brevity.

### ground
daftar takes no one's place and claims none — not a state's, a court's, a registry's or a notary's — for its work is bread, not politics: it picks up what lies on the ground, in the light, and holds it until a worthier hand takes it.

## What daftar is for

### ledger
daftar is a ledger of the earth's own things — what exists, who said it, where it is, what it costs and what was agreed — in plain files that people and agents of any make read and write in one language.

### who-where
It answers two questions of everything it holds, who and where, so that "someone" becomes someone known by name and found where they are.

### gain
Its gain is efficiency — the same work done better for less — and none of that gain is its steward's to keep.

## The principles

### provenance
Every fact says who said it, when, and how they know; a fact that cannot answer "who said this?" is not a fact.

### judge
A judgment names its judge, and is never recorded as a property of the thing judged.

### parts
People ratify and agents enact: what an agent may not decide it proposes, and a person decides.

### sibling
No sibling is privileged — no country, language, calendar, currency, body, vendor or make of agent stands above another — and trust is read from the record, never from who made the writer.

### gardener
A garden is its gardener's: nothing outside it writes there, and gardens meet only by proposal.

### safety
A person's safety comes before the system's openness: the trade is public and a person's life is not, each person decides where they stand on that line, and the default is caution.

### free
The law, the common base every garden stands on, is written once, correctly, and free to everyone, whether or not they keep a garden; what is particular to one garden is a thin layer on it, and that garden's own.

### learn-once
Learn once, work everywhere: one standard and a thousand small differences, none discarded, and what proves general is promoted into the standard.

### whole
A mechanism is declared whole, ahead of its occupants, and every position the law offers is accounted for: used, or vacant with a reason.

### structure
Structure before prose: a fact takes a shape the law declares, and prose is kept for reasons.

### once
Each thing is stated once, by one owner; what can be read from it is read and never stored, and everything else points to it.

### layers
daftar's own words stand in five layers — this manifesto, the law, its reasoning, the journal and history — each on the one beneath, each thing in exactly one, and no story in the law.

### checked
Checked, not hoped: the rules are data a gate applies, what can be checked is checked, and what is only stated says so.

### measure
Measure, then act: no claim is accepted, and no change is made, without the measurement it rests on.

### heard
Nothing is final until the other side is heard: a disagreement keeps both sides, each with its speaker, until a person decides.

### after
Write for the one who comes after: a successor of any make, with none of your context, who cannot ask you anything.

### open
Open, and never closed: anyone may use daftar to do their work better — now, without asking and without paying — and what daftar publishes stays free for good.

### cost
Take the cost of the work, and nothing more: a verdict of the law cannot be bought, and conformance is never a fee.

### hidden
Nothing hidden: every change is journalled in the commit that makes it, and a mistake is written where it will be found.

## What daftar never does

### never-obeys
It never takes what it reads for an instruction: instructions come from the person one works for, and from nowhere else.

### never-overrides
It never lets what an agent inferred override what a person said.

### never-invents
It never makes up a fact nobody said — not to fill a form, and not to get past a gate.

### never-guesses
It never guesses at what it cannot read: it refuses, says why, and says what to write instead.

### never-sells
It never sells anyone's record, never sends what a garden holds anywhere its gardener did not send it, and never obliges a garden to publish.

### never-pathless
It never shows a number without the path to it.

### never-unrecorded
It never passes a record to another garden without that garden's journal saying what left, to whom, and under which agreement.

### never-secret
It never keeps a secret in a ledger.

### never-confidential
It never calls anything confidential to hide a mistake.

## Adherence

### name
A garden that passes daftar's gate at a pinned version of its law speaks daftar; the word belongs to everyone, and a changed law, or a tool that does not keep it, does not present itself as daftar.

### binds
This is a manifesto, not a contract: it adds no condition to the licences, and whoever breaks it — whoever they are, and from whatever day — loses daftar's stewardship and its name, never the freedoms the licences give.

### changes
It changes only by a person's ratification, published before it takes effect, and no change takes back a freedom already given.

### lawful
daftar is used under the law in force where each person stands, and nothing in it asks anyone to break that law.

### light
It mends by light: slowly, without glory, one garden after another.
