Money between two people

Sam and Ali share a camera, and over dinner Sam lends Ali the price of a washing machine, to be repaid in six monthly instalments. His bank's statement proves the payment. The notebook records what moved, what was agreed and where, and keeps the proof — and what one owes the other is read from all of it, exactly, never written down beside it.

What Sam says, and what he reads

Orient — for the person. Sam never opens a terminal. Over six weeks he tells his agent what happened, in his own words, and reads what it did. The conversation is written for this page; every figure, date and refusal the agent reports in it is what the tools print under Operate.

  1. 10 September — a camera.
  2. Sam · spoken

    Ali and I bought a camera. She paid for it — ninety XTS. I use it more, so we said I'd carry two thirds.

  3. Sam's agent

    Written as one agreement between you and Ali, owned by neither of you: 90 XTS on 10 September, paid by Ali, borne two parts by you and one by her. The notebook's check passed, and its journal says what I did. You owe Ali 60 XTS — read from the payment each time you ask, never stored to go stale.

  4. 12 September — a dinner, and a loan.
  5. Sam · spoken

    Ali came to dinner at mine, half past seven. We agreed I'd lend her the money for her washing machine — nothing written, we just said it. I paid the shop 120 the next day. She pays me back 20 on the first of each month, six times; a late one carries one percent a month.

  6. Sam's agent

    Does she start on the first of October?

  7. Sam

    Yes.

  8. Sam's agent

    Two pages. The dinner, as a happening: 12 September 2026 at 19:30, you the host and Ali present — in the Persian calendar, 21 Shahrivar 1405. And the loan: the 120 XTS you paid the shop, with two clauses — 20 XTS on the first of each month, six times from 1 October, and one percent a month on an instalment paid late. Its words were spoken, at that dinner.

  9. A week later — the proof.
  10. Sam · typed

    Here is my card statement for September — the PDF. Keep it, but not my card number.

  11. Sam's agent

    Kept as a document whose identity is its content: if a single byte of it changes, it is another document. I copied the one line that matters — 13 September, the appliance shop, 120.00 XTS — with the card number left out but its last four digits.

    One mistake was caught on the way: I first wrote the file's fingerprint in capital letters, as Windows prints it, and the notebook refused it — it keeps one spelling only, so that two notebooks recognise the same document. Written again, and saved.

  12. 1 October, and after.
  13. Sam · typed

    Ali paid the first 20 today.

  14. Sam's agent

    Recorded, beside the loan — not as a new balance.

  15. Sam · 27 October

    Where do Ali and I stand?

  16. Sam's agent

    You owe her 60 XTS for the camera; she owes you 100 for the loan. Net, Ali owes you 40 XTS. Her next instalment is due on 1 November — in 5 days.

  17. XTS is the code ISO keeps for testing. Your own notebook writes your own currency's code.

What the agent does underneath

Understand — for the curious. Between Sam's words and its answers, the agent calls the notebook's own tools. The same few moves carry the whole story: write a page, say why, save it through the gate, read the answer. Each in plain words first, then exactly, with what the tool printed.

  1. write It writes one page for each thing

    The camera agreement, the dinner, the loan and the statement are four files, each readable on its own.

    Exactly: beans/shared-camera.md and washer-loan.md are contracts, dinner-at-sams.md an event, card-statement-2026-09.md a document anchored by the hash of its bytes — each in the shape of the notebook's recipe, amounts as exact strings. They are shown whole under Inspect.

The statement that proves it, its secret left out

What the notebook records

Inspect — for the auditor. Exactly what was written, and why it is shaped so.

A camera, its cost shared

An agreement is a contract bean. This one is owned by neither of them: it ends at the crown, where every chain of ownership ends, in its branch logos — the word, for what exists by being said and agreed — and both parties answer for it. A party with accepted said yes on that day. The transaction says what moved: an amount, the day, who paid, and who bears it in whole-number shares.

The statement that proves it, its secret left out

The bank wrote the statement, so the bank owns it; Sam holds the copy, and located_at says where it is. content_hash is the SHA-256 of the file's bytes. The lines are a capture: never authoritative, keyed by what would make it stale, and carrying no secret — redactions says what was left out, and why. taken_at is the moment it was taken, in milliseconds.

What it shows

  • What is owed is read, never written. There is no balance to store: a stored balance is a second copy of what the transactions say, and it drifts the first time a transaction is added and the balance is not.
  • Nothing is rounded. Every share is a fraction. One that does not come out even is shown as the fraction it is, and who takes the remainder is something the parties agree, in a clause.
  • No currency is privileged. Every amount stays in the currency it was paid in; no factor joins two currencies, and a rate is an observation someone made. No privileged sibling.
  • An agreement between two people may be owned by neither. Then both answer for it, and the two notebooks' records of it agree about its owner. The crown.
