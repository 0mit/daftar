Two people's notebooks meet

Ali keeps a notebook of her own, and nothing outside it writes there — not Sam, and not Sam's agent. Sam's garden lays a proposal beside both, under an agreement both are parties to. Ali's garden reads it, takes it into the working tree, and her commit is what lets it in. Taking the agreement records what Sam's garden offers; her yes is hers to write.

garden-sam and garden-ali · daftar v0.34.0 · the day held at 2026-10-27

What the notebooks record

A garden is known by the commit it grew from. Each gardener runs dmpropose id and tells the other what it prints:

~/garden-sam$ python3 bin/dmpropose.py id
garden_id: "e3b0cd6eb9ba"
garden: garden-sam
gardener: sam
~/garden-ali$ python3 bin/dmpropose.py id
garden_id: "de0350c43f85"
garden: garden-ali
gardener: ali

First contact, on Sam's side: a garden bean anchored by the id Ali read out, owned by her and answered for by her — so Ali is a person bean here too, named as her own garden names her. No bean owns a person: hers ends at the crown's branch agape, love that does not possess, and she answers for herself (the words). The two are one commit, and the gardener's decision:

What it shows

  • Gardens meet only by proposal. What passes is one file, never a write; reading it writes nothing, taking it commits nothing, and the gardener's commit is the ratification. The mycelium.
  • Taking is not accepting. Taking an agreement records what the other garden offers. A party's acceptance is its own word, written by its own garden.
  • A name is minted once, and carried. The garden that recorded the camera first names it, and Ali's garden keeps the name byte for byte, so the two notebooks see one agreement, not two.
  • Consent comes first. A proposal is made under an agreement both gardeners are parties to.
