Two people's notebooks meet

Ali keeps a notebook of her own, and nothing outside it writes there — not Sam, and not Sam's agent. Sam's garden lays a proposal beside both, under an agreement both are parties to. Ali's garden reads it, takes it into the working tree, and her commit is what lets it in. Taking the agreement records what Sam's garden offers; her yes is hers to write.

What Sam and Ali say, and what they read

Orient — for the two people. Each talks to their own agent, in their own notebook. Neither agent writes in the other's. The conversations are written for this page; what the agents report is what the tools print under Operate.

  1. In Sam's notebook.
  2. Sam · typed

    Send Ali the camera agreement, so her notebook has it too.

  3. Sam's agent

    Her notebook is hers: I cannot write in it, and nothing should. I have made a proposal instead — one file, laid beside both notebooks, offering the camera agreement under the agreement itself, which you are both party to. The file carries a fingerprint, so her side can tell if a line of it was changed.

    Nothing has been sent. Give her the file however you like; your journal already says what left, to whom, and under which agreement.

  4. In Ali's notebook.
  5. Ali

    Sam gave me a file from his notebook. What is it?

  6. Ali's agent

    A proposal from Sam's notebook, made on 27 October. Its fingerprint checks: nobody changed a line of it. Both notebooks keep the same law, so it can be read here. It offers the camera — 90 XTS you paid, borne two parts by Sam and one by you. But your notebook has not met Sam's yet. May I write him in, and his notebook, first?

  7. Ali

    Yes.

  8. Ali's agent

    Done, and read again: it passes your notebook's check. I have taken it in — your notebook now holds the camera agreement under the name Sam's gave it, and reads the same debt: Sam owes you 60 XTS.

    Taking it in accepted nothing for you. It says you agreed, but that is Sam's record. Do you agree?

  9. Ali

    I do.

  10. Ali's agent

    Written: your own yes, in your own words and your own commit. If the same file comes again, it will be refused: it was taken already.

  11. Before the loan crosses — trying it first.
  12. Sam

    Before the washing-machine loan goes to Ali for real, can we try it?

  13. Sam's agent

    I have grown a rehearsal notebook — a notebook of its own that says, in its own first page, that it is a rehearsal — and made the proposal from there. Ali's side can read it and see exactly what would happen.

  14. Ali's agent

    This one comes from a rehearsal. It reads cleanly, and it would pass your notebook's check — but your notebook will not take it in as fact: a rehearsal never passes for anyone's word. I can take it into a scratch copy, marked as a rehearsal, if you want to see it there.

What it shows

  • Gardens meet only by proposal. What passes is one file, never a write; reading it writes nothing, taking it commits nothing, and the gardener's commit is the ratification. The mycelium.
  • Taking is not accepting. Taking an agreement records what the other garden offers. A party's acceptance is its own word, written by its own garden.
  • A name is minted once, and carried. The garden that recorded the camera first names it, and Ali's garden keeps the name byte for byte, so the two notebooks see one agreement, not two.
  • Consent comes first. A proposal is made under an agreement both gardeners are parties to.
  • A rehearsal is grown, never copied. A clone of a real garden carries its id, and what it proposed would be that garden's word.
  • Each gardener marks it in their own records. The rehearsal says so in its manifest; the receiving garden says so on its own garden bean, and holds later proposals to it whatever they say.
  • A rehearsal never passes for a person's word. A garden that is not a test garden takes one in only with --as-test, and then every bean it writes says so.
