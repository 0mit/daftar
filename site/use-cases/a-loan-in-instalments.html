A loan repaid in instalments

Over dinner, Sam lent Ali the price of a washing machine, to be repaid in six monthly instalments, with interest on one paid late. The clauses say what each owes and when; the transactions say what moved. The tools read what is owed and what falls due next.

garden-sam · daftar v0.34.0 · the day held at 2026-10-27

What the notebook records

Each clause says who it binds, whom it is owed to, how much, from when, and how it repeats; unless it says otherwise (stance), it is required. The late interest is not brought into force by a date, so it says what brings it: when. The words were spoken, at the dinner:

What it shows

  • Payments are not yet matched to occurrences. dmledger reads what the payments add up to, and takes the next day, and which occurrence it is, from the calendar alone. It does not say that an instalment was missed or paid late, and nothing computes the late interest: that clause is listed as in force when its condition holds, and a person reads the rest from the transactions.
  • A clause says what became of it. Its state — met, waived, broken, disputed — is written the day something becomes of it, and dmstale stops warning once it says met, waived or broken. Declared whole.
  • A repetition names its calendar. “Each month” means nothing until it says whose month: here gregorian-civil. No privileged sibling.
