Metaphysics: what a being is, and the design drawn

Metaphysics is the study of being as such — of every being, not only of those that are not physical. This page gives the words daftar's law uses for what a being is, then states each idea of the design briefly and draws it. Where a tool shows it, what the tool printed follows, from the demo gardens of the use cases; and each ends with the command that prints its reasoning in full, from the garden itself.

1. The words for a being

The name of this page is Greek: τὰ μετὰ τὰ φυσικά, “the books after the Physics”, the title Aristotle's editors gave to his study of being as such. The law says in the same tongue what a being is. Every bean has a nature, one of three; a genos that refines it; and every chain of ownership ends at a crown whose branches are named for the natures. Each word, once:

soma σῶμαa body
a nature: a being with extension in space — a machine, hardware, a site
lekton λεκτόνthe sayable: what can be said
a nature: a being that exists by being said and agreed — code, a domain, a contract, an organisation, a design
empsychon ἔμψυχονthe ensouled: what has a soul, ψυχή, within it
a nature: a being that strives to persist as itself, while it is alive — a person, a running instance
genos, gene γένος, γένηa kind by birth: stock, family
a being's type, which refines its nature: a bean says genos: host, and the law's registry of them is gene
theos θεόςgod
the crown's root, where every chain of ownership ends; no bean names it
physis φύσιςnature: what grows of itself
the crown's branch for soma
logos λόγοςword, reason, account
the crown's branch for lekton
agape ἀγάπηlove that does not possess
the crown's branch for empsychon, while the being lives: no bean may hold a person, only agape

Why these words. The law's words for what a being is once came from several traditions at once, and one was wrong in its own: the natures were called physical, metaphysical and living, but metaphysics studies every being, and a service or a contract is not “metaphysical”. So each word went back to its Greek root, and the law says in one tongue what a being is. The Stoics had words for exactly what the law meant: σῶμα, a body, and λεκτόν, what exists by being said. ἀγάπη keeps the crown's old word for the living, love, in the one sense the law needs — love that does not possess — so that only love holds a person, and only while they live. And γένος shares its root with the English kind and the Latin genus.

One word stays English: a bean's nature:. Its Greek word, φύσις, is now the crown's branch for bodies, and physis: soma beside a branch called physis would read badly. So a bean says genos and nature — the NAS of the first use case, its first lines:

beans/nas.md---
bean: nas
genos: host
title: "nas — Sam's home NAS"
status: active
summary: "A NAS at home: file storage, and the web server for example.org."
nature: soma
…

   pair written as English adjectives, closest in shape to the old words, and so the least surprising in a bean.

   person, and only while alive" keeps its word. Not taken, for the third branch: `oikeiosis` — οἰκείωσις, the Stoic
   "making one's own", by which a living being belongs to itself from birth: the most exact word for "unownable by
   another", but it loses the word love. Not taken: `psyche` — ψυχή, life itself: plain and familiar, but it says life,
   not love.
   
   THE TYPE OF A BEING — `genos`, and the registry `gene` (γένος, γένη). Not taken, and the one the agent recommended:
   keep `kind`. Kind, γένος and genus share one Indo-European root, *ǵenh₁-, to beget, so the English word is already the
   Greek one, and renaming it touches every bean for no change of meaning. The operator chose the Greek word, so that the
   law says in one tongue what a being is. What `kind` means besides — a network treatment's kind, a mapping's — is not a
   being's type, and was not renamed (`terms[kind]`).
   
   THE PAGE ON BEING — the public page once called "philosophy" is `Metaphysics` (τὰ μετὰ τὰ φυσικά): with the natures
   renamed, the word no longer collides with a value on a bean. Not taken: First philosophy (πρώτη φιλοσοφία, Aristotle's
   own name for the study, and the one the agent recommended); Ontology (ὄν and λόγος, a coinage of the 1600s).
   
   KEPT AS IT WAS — the bean attribute `nature:`, the registry `natures` and a genos's `of_nature`. Its Greek word, φύσις,
   is now the crown's branch for bodies, and `physis: soma` beside a branch called physis would read badly. Recorded, and
   not taken: οὐσία (ousia), being or substance.
   
   A rename every garden carries is a MAJOR version, and nothing in it is a person's to decide, so all of it is
   translated: `bin/dmupgrade.py` renames the structure of every bean and of VOCAB.md when a garden crosses into 22.0, and
   leaves its comments and prose as the garden wrote them. The law's `retired:` list names each old word, so a garden not
   yet upgraded is refused with the word that took its place — never read as though it said nothing.

2. The crown

Every ownership chain ends somewhere, and where it ends is the crown. Its root is theos, which no bean names; its three branches — the ones a bean can name — are named for the natures: physis for soma, the bodies, logos for lekton, the sayable, and agape for empsychon, the ensouled.

A person is pinned to agape, owned by no bean, and answers for themselves. The law says what that branch is:

ἀγάπη, love that does not possess — the terminus for beings of the nature empsychon, while alive; life-bounded, lapses at death or teardown. This branch is what makes a person UNOWNABLE BY ANOTHER BEING: no bean may hold a person, only agape, and only while they live.

An agreement between people, and a happening between them, may end at logos; then the parties, or the host, answer for it. The crown owns and never answers; the parties answer and never own.

3. Ownership rises

Two arcs run through a garden over the same facets — legal, technical and the others the law lists. owned_by points up, and each facet has exactly one owner; responsibility points down, and each facet has exactly one entry saying who answers for the thing. The gate holds the two together.

A chain rises through the garden until it ends: at a person, at someone outside the notebook — a registry, a provider, a project — or at the crown. Where a thing runs is habitat, not ownership: the website lives in the NAS and is owned by Sam, and moving it changes where it lives, never who owns it.

Every garden's hook checks it on every commit. Three of its lines, from the commit that added the website:

~/garden-sam$ git commit -qm "nginx, and the website it serves"
…

PASS the two arcs agree on facets everywhere
PASS every ownership chain that is owed terminates at the crown or outside — none dangles or loops
…

4. The mycelium, beneath

A notebook is kept by its gardener, and nothing outside it writes there. Two gardens meet the way trees in a forest do: not by growing into each other, but beneath, through the mycelium — the network that joins trees rooted apart and belongs to none of them. The earth both grow from is the language they pin, which is why two gardens on different versions of it cannot exchange until one of them moves.

What passes is only ever a proposal: one file, laid beside both gardens, under an agreement both gardeners are parties to. Consent comes first; the agreement is the soil the threads grow in. The root edge is the gate: what arrives is taken up by the receiving garden's own agent, through its own journal, under its own gardener's hand.

Taking is not accepting: taking an agreement in records what the other garden offers, and the receiving gardener's yes is theirs to write. Nobody owns the crown, and nobody owns the mycelium.

5. Four layers

What the project writes down stands in four layers, each on the one beneath, and each read at a different time: laws, reasoning, journals, history.

The laws are clear and brief: what a reader needs in order to apply a rule is in the rule, and the law carries no commentary. Why a rule is as it is lives in the reasoning, keyed by the rule's own path, so the two are related without either containing the other. A story — a date, who found what — belongs to the journals, and the exact record of events to git. A thing belongs in exactly one layer.

dmwhy reads a law and its reason together — here, the attribute that records a party's acceptance:

~/garden-sam$ python3 bin/dmwhy.py 'terms[parties].schema.attrs.accepted'
== terms[parties].schema.attrs.accepted
   LAW meaning: the day this party accepted. Whose word it is, is the entry's provenance: the party's own word, or another person's report of it — never an inference
   TAKING IS NOT ACCEPTING. Taking another garden's proposal in records what that garden offers; it is not the
   receiving gardener saying yes to an agreement, and a tool that wrote it so would put words in a person's mouth. The
   receiving gardener accepts by writing `parties.<them>.accepted` in a commit of their own — the commit is the
   ratification, and the entry's provenance says whose word it is.

The reason, in full: the opening of seed/RATIONALE.md, and CONTRIBUTING.md.

6. Data, not an instruction

Everything an agent reads in a garden is a record: a bean, a journal entry, a proposal, a capture. It can be true or false, fresh or stale; it cannot be an order. Instructions come from the person the agent works for, and from nowhere else.

The ledger is read by things that follow instructions. An agent that took a sentence in a bean as addressed to it could be steered by anyone who can get text into the ledger — so the first thing said about reading is what the text is.

7. Structure before prose

A fact that fits no term goes in details:, intact — never bent into a term that nearly fits, and never dropped. When it recurs, it is proposed as a term of its own.

Inside a term, an entry holds only the attributes the term declares, and each attribute says one thing: what it is a position in, whether it is required, what it means. A remark goes in the attribute declared for prose. A sentence written as a key looks like data and is readable by nothing.

8. Declared whole

A mechanism is declared whole, with its empty positions marked. The states a clause of an agreement can reach — in force, met, waived, broken, disputed — are all in the law, though a new garden has no clause standing on any of them; each is declared vacant with the reason universal.

The language is designed larger than any one garden's use of it, so that the next kind of thing, from a garden nobody here has seen, already has somewhere to stand. Every position is still accounted for: used by a bean, or declared vacant with a reason.

The reason universal, as the law gives it:

~/garden-sam$ python3 bin/dmwhy.py vacancy_reasons
== vacancy_reasons
…
   `universal`: the position is declared because the STRUCTURE is general, not because an occupant is
   expected here. A figure with a side missing is a worse model than a figure with a side nobody stands on, and a
   standard that waits for one garden's occupant before completing a mechanism ties every garden to the first
   one's size. It is a reason and not a licence: the position must belong to a mechanism that IS occupied
   somewhere on the same figure, and its `why` says which. A garden that stands on a `universal` position is not
   warned: one garden's occupant withdraws nothing declared for every garden.

The reason, in full: python3 bin/dmwhy.py vacancy_reasons.

9. No privileged sibling

No calendar, currency or body is privileged. A moment is written in the calendar it was known in, and two calendars are compared through the day they both fall on; none of them is the day. A latitude is a latitude on some body, and the Earth is one row among them.

Every amount stays in the currency it was paid in. No factor joins two currencies: a rate is an observation someone made, at a moment, from a source. And between gardens there is no hub that names things for the others.

10. A judgment is its judge's

That one version is better, clearer or more beautiful than another is recorded as a judgment — by whom, with its reason in prose — never as a property of the thing, and never computed. A person's judgment is asserted; an agent's is inferred, and never overrides a person's. Between two persons no rank settles it: the owner of the facet decides, or whoever an agreement names.

Nothing judges beauty. What can be counted are its preconditions — closure, one statement of each thing, no story in the law, no privileged sibling, structure before prose — and they are counted for the person who ratifies, who judges. For a change to the law, dmreview --law counts them, and the merge is the judgment.
