Contribute: how to ask

The vocabulary grows because gardens bring it cases it had not met. Each way of asking below is a form on the project's issue tracker. Every form asks for the situation in your own words, and none asks for anything from your garden.

Ask

  • A need

    Something you want to be able to record, or to ask of your notebook, and cannot yet.

  • A use case

    A situation you keep a record of, or would: the things involved, and what you want the notebook to say about them.

  • A suggestion

    A change to a rule, a tool, a document or this site, with the case that showed it.

  • A question

    Something the documents did not answer, and what you read first.

  • A bug

    A tool did something other than what it says it does: the command, what it printed, and what you expected.

Or browse the open issues, or choose a form.

What the forms ask for, and what they never ask for

They ask for the situation, in your own words. They never ask for your estate — no host names, addresses, paths, people's names, ids, serials or findings. Write neutral names instead: sam, ali, host-a, example.org, 203.0.113.10, /home/user/…. A garden is private; this repository is not, and what you paste into an issue is public. Every form says so, and asks you to confirm it.

How a need becomes law

A change to the vocabulary or a rule is a change to the law every garden pins, so it is proposed, shown beside its neighbours, and ratified by a person. CONTRIBUTING.md says how; in short:

  1. A pull request is a proposal, and merging it is the ratification. Anyone may propose; a person ratifies, and the change is recorded distinctly as a rule-change.

  2. Say which of two things you are proposing, because they are judged differently. A term for a kind of fact starts in your garden's own VOCAB.md, used by real beans, and is promoted once it has held real cases. A mechanism may be proposed whole, ahead of its occupants, if it is universal, general, canonical, useful and simple to read, with its empty positions declared universal.

  3. Bring evidence, and see the neighbourhood. For any change to the law, paste what python3 bin/dmreview.py --law --against <the tag you started from> prints. It counts what the change adds, removes, restates and narrates, and judges nothing; the person who merges judges.

  4. A release follows. Merged changes reach gardens in the next release tag, and each garden adopts it with bin/dmupgrade.py when its own gardener decides to.

Before a pull request

  • Run the tests: CONTRIBUTING.md lists them, one command a line, the same in a Unix shell and in PowerShell.
  • Run bin/dmpublic.py over your text, the pull request's body included: it refuses anything that names your garden's estate, from the names your garden itself holds.
  • Leave your estate out of it, in the prose around the change as much as in the change.
